# Methodology — the GRPO fuzzer

## 1. The fuzzer as an RL problem

AFL++ runs a coverage-guided loop over a token queue (TLAFL/CovRL u16 seeds,
decoded only at execution). We treat the LLM mutator as a stochastic policy and
learn it from the interpreter's own feedback, as CovRL-Fuzz does — but with
**GRPO** (group-relative, critic-free) in place of CovRL's PPO + learned rewarder.

- **Prompt / state**: an unmasked seed `x` selected from the AFL queue.
- **Action**: an infill `y` of a **random** span mask `m` of `x` (`m` is sampled,
  not learned — see §2).
- **Step**: reconstruct `x' = splice(x, m, y)`, execute on the target, observe the
  coverage bitmap and validity.
- **Reward** (CovRL Eq. 2/5): `r = −1` syntax error, `−0.5` semantic error, `−1`
  timeout, else `b + (1−b)·R_cov` for a valid mutant, with
  `R_cov = σ(log Σ_e tf_e·idf_e)` over the edges the mutant adds **vs its parent
  seed** (delta-vs-parent; no-new-coverage → 0). A black-box scalar to the learner.
  Full reward analysis: [coverage_signal.md](coverage_signal.md).

It is a **contextual bandit** (one decision per mutant), not a multi-step MDP: no
bootstrapping, the return is the immediate `r`.

## 2. Policy — random masking + GRPO infiller

The policy is the **span-denoising infiller** of one CodeT5p-220m (`π_θ(y|x,m)`);
the mask `m` is **random** span corruption (CovRL/T5-style, `data/masking.py`), not
a learned head. (A learned encoder-masker was tried and **abandoned** — it
collapsed to no-op/identity mutations; the bi-level machinery is gone from the
code.) Per seed the mutator emits **M random masks × G infills** in block layout —
each mask's `G` infills form one GRPO group.

## 3. GRPO objective

Per group (the `G` infills of one mask), critic-free, within-group baseline:

```
A_i = ( r_i − mean_g r ) / std_g                 z-scored within the mask group
L   = − E_i[ clip(ρ_i,1±ε)·A_i ]  +  κ·KL(π‖π_rollout)  +  κ_ref·KL(π‖π_snapshot)
```

- **ρ ≡ 1** at the single on-policy step (one optimizer step per cycle, dropout
  off), so the clip and the `κ`-KL-vs-rollout are **inert today** (kept correct for
  a future multi-step opt-in). The live gradient is `−E[A·∇log π]` — pure GRPO.
- **The anchor `κ_ref·KL(π‖π_snapshot)` is the load-bearing stability term**: a
  per-token k3 KL to a **frozen snapshot** of the policy (refreshed every
  `ref_update_every` cycles), which holds the policy on the valid manifold and
  stops the entropy random-walk that otherwise collapses it. Anchoring to *base*
  blows up (the policy must diverge from base to fuzz → the k3 `exp` explodes); a
  policy-*tracking* snapshot keeps the KL small and bounded. See §5.
- **No CE/MLM auxiliary.** Off-policy CE on a fixed corpus doesn't compose with the
  z-scored on-policy advantage. Validity is shaped through the reward (the −1/−0.5
  floor) + the anchor, not a separate loss.

## 4. Dataset dynamics (the defining property)

The training set is **generated by the policy online and is non-stationary** — this
is the hard part. Most of this section is now validated empirically (see
[coverage_signal.md](coverage_signal.md) and the run records).

- **Online assembly per cycle.** A *cycle* = `finetune_every` queue entries fuzzed
  (CovRL `SYNC_INTERVAL=100`). Each `x` yields `M·G` samples `(x,m,y,r)`; their
  union is the GRPO batch, trained once at the cycle boundary. On-policy within a
  cycle.
- **Self-generated, drifting seed distribution.** AFL's scheduler picks `x`
  (favored/fast-biased), and interesting mutants are **re-queued**
  (`save_if_interesting`) → they become future `x` — the *mutation-of-mutation*
  loop. The queue grows unboundedly (one run: 100 → 7358 entries, lineage depth 17)
  and never completes an AFL cycle (it grows faster than it is swept). AFL is
  validity-blind, so coverage-novel **syntax errors** are re-queued too →
  **corpus rot**: the prompt distribution drifts toward invalid seeds.
- **Drifting reward.** Coverage is monotone-saturating — early mutants hit new
  edges easily (dense `R_cov`), then the frontier hardens (sparse; the AFL
  *scheduler* carries late coverage, not the per-infill reward). TF-IDF compounds
  it: a path's `idf` falls as its document frequency rises (momentum
  `idf_t = α·idf_{t−1} + (1−α)·idf_new`), so identical coverage pays **less over
  time** — an implicit explore-then-refine curriculum.
- **Group composition gates the gradient.** GRPO advantage is within-group; an
  all-equal group has zero advantage → no signal. Two failure modes follow, and
  they are **orthogonal**:
  - *coverage-inert* — absolute `R_cov` is ~constant across one mask's infills
    (action-independent ⇒ cancelled by the within-group baseline). Fix:
    **delta-vs-parent** coverage, which makes `R_cov` vary with the infill.
  - *validity-collapse* — low validity ⇒ all-invalid (dead) groups ⇒ sparse
    gradient ⇒ the unanchored policy random-walks to high-entropy garbage. Fix: the
    **KL snapshot anchor** (model side) + an **adequately sized corpus** (input
    side).
- **Co-adaptation.** The reward surface moves under the policy's own success
  (re-queue + idf decay), so it is a moving target by construction; on-policy
  re-rollout each cycle tracks it.

## 5. What makes it stable (validated)

A long run (JerryScript 3.0.0, 104 cycles / 8.4 h, single RTX 3090) is stable and
productive with **three orthogonal levers, all required**:

1. **delta-vs-parent coverage** (`cfg.delta_coverage`) — the *coverage* signal
   (within-group variance; ~800× the absolute reward's, measured).
2. **per-token KL snapshot anchor** (`cfg.kl_ref_coef`, `ref_update_every`) —
   *model* stability (bounded KL, no entropy random-walk).
3. **adequately sized corpus** (≈100 valid seeds, not 10) — *input* stability:
   corpus rot is a transient dip that **recovers**, not a death spiral.

Result: `bitmap_cvg` 38→58.5%, validity dips then recovers to ~50%, `kl_ref`
bounded throughout. Without the anchor the policy collapses (~cycle 34, entropy
0.9→2.6); without delta the coverage gradient is ~0; on a 10-seed corpus validity
collapses to ~0 (rot). Full analysis: [coverage_signal.md](coverage_signal.md).
